Male customers outnumber female customers in all cities, with Mumbai having the highest and Hyderabad the lowest overall customer count.

The chart shows that the 25–34 age group has the highest number of customers, especially males. Across all age groups, male customers consistently outnumber female customers, with the fewest customers in the 45+ age group.

![Screenshot (210)](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/8388d7de-3efc-4557-82bc-f48efe013426)

The chart shows that male customers spend significantly more than female customers across all categories. The highest male spending is on Bills (81M) and Groceries (66M), while females spend most on Health & Wellness (29M). Overall, males dominate spending in every category.

The chart indicates that male customers outnumber female customers across all occupations. Salaried IT Employees have the highest customer count for both genders, while Business Owners and Freelancers show a particularly large male dominance.

Spend Analysis of Customers

![Screenshot (209)](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/0e45874e-556c-4cd3-bc2b-b98dd749275e)

The chart shows that **Salaried IT Employees** have the highest total income (0.48bn) and spending (0.24bn), with an income utilization rate of **51.04%**, the highest among all occupations. **Government Employees** have the lowest income (0.12bn), spend the least (0.04bn), and also have the **lowest income utilization rate (29%)**. **Business Owners** have a relatively high income but the **lowest utilization rate (33.22%)**, suggesting they spend conservatively relative to their earnings.
